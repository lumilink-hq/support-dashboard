--- title: How to Show Up in ChatGPT and Google's AI Overviews description: AI answers pull from the same web your customers search. Six practical steps to get your business cited, and how to check whether it worked. date: 2026-10-08 ---

A customer who wants a plumber, a dentist or a new pair of running shoes might never see your website. They might type the question into ChatGPT, or read the AI Overview at the top of Google, and pick from the two or three businesses the answer names. If your business isn't one of them, you lost that customer before they reached a search result.

You can't buy a place in these answers. Both tools build them from pages they can find, read and trust, so most of the work sits with you, and most of it also helps you rank in ordinary search.

How AI Answers Choose Their Sources

ChatGPT can search the web while it writes an answer, and it links to the pages it used. Google's AI Overviews draw on pages in Google's own index. Google's guidance for site owners says a page needs to be indexed and eligible to show a snippet in regular search before it can appear as a supporting link in an AI Overview.

That gives you two jobs. Make sure these tools can reach your pages, then make sure your pages answer your customers' questions clearly enough to quote.

1. Let the Crawlers In

Open your site's robots.txt file and check what it blocks. OpenAI uses a crawler called OAI-SearchBot to find pages for ChatGPT's search answers, and it follows robots.txt. If your file blocks it, your pages can't appear as cited sources there. Google's AI Overviews rely on Googlebot, the same crawler behind regular search.

Some website builders and security plugins block AI crawlers by default. Check your settings and allow the crawlers you want to reach you.

2. Make Sure Google Has Indexed Your Important Pages

Google Search Console shows which of your pages Google has indexed and why it skipped the rest. Submit a sitemap, then look at the pages that matter most: your homepage, each service or product page, and each location page. A page that Google hasn't indexed can't appear in an AI Overview.

Fix the common blockers first:

  • Pages marked noindex by mistake, often left over from a site redesign.
  • Pages that only load their text after a script runs, so a crawler sees an empty shell.
  • Several URLs showing the same content without a canonical tag that names the main one.

3. Answer the Questions Customers Ask, in Plain Words

AI answers quote text that responds to a question directly. Write down the ten questions your staff hear most often, by phone, by email and at the counter. Then make sure your site answers each one on a page where it belongs.

Put the answer near the top of its section, in a sentence or two, before the detail. "We repair tankless water heaters from all major brands, usually the same day in Orange County" gives an AI tool something to quote. A paragraph about your passion for quality gives it nothing.

Use the words your customers use. If people ask for "emergency AC repair", say that, not "after-hours climate solutions".

4. Keep Your Business Details Consistent Everywhere

Your name, address, phone number, hours and service area should match across your website, your Google Business Profile, Yelp, Apple Maps, Facebook and the directories in your industry. Customers and AI tools both read those listings, and conflicting hours or an old address give either one a reason to doubt the rest.

Spend an hour searching your business name and fixing every listing that disagrees with your website.

5. Add Structured Data

Structured data is a short block of code, invisible to visitors, that states facts about your business in a format machines read without guessing: your business type, address, hours, services and prices. Schema.org defines the vocabulary. Google uses it to understand pages, and it costs little to add.

Most website builders and SEO plugins can add the basic LocalBusiness or Organization markup for you. Test the result with Google's Rich Results Test or the Schema.org validator, and make sure every fact in the markup matches what the page shows.

6. Earn Mentions on Other Sites

An AI answer that recommends a business usually draws on more than that business's own website. Reviews, local news coverage, industry directories and "best of" lists all tell these tools that real people know and use you.

Ask happy customers for reviews, and reply to the reviews you get. Join your local chamber of commerce or trade association and make sure their member page lists you. Pitch a local reporter when you do something worth covering.

How to Check Whether It's Working

Pick five to ten questions a customer would ask before choosing a business like yours. "Best emergency plumber in Irvine" or "where can I buy trail running shoes near Costa Mesa" both work. Each week, ask ChatGPT and Google those exact questions and note:

  • whether the answer mentions your business,
  • whether it links to your site,
  • and which competitors it names instead.

The answers change from week to week, so one check tells you little. A few months of notes show you a trend, and the competitor names show you whose pages the AI tools trust for your questions. Study those pages. They usually answer the question more directly than yours do.
